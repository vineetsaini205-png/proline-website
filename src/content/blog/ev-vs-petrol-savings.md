An electric car charged at home costs about ₹1.14 a kilometre to run, against ₹6.81 for a petrol car, so at 40 km a day it saves roughly ₹6,798 a month, or ₹81,578 a year, on running costs. That uses Delhi's petrol price of ₹102.12 a litre on 20 October 2026 and home electricity at ₹8 a unit; scooters, SUVs, autos and cabs save too, by different amounts.

This guide shows the sums behind those numbers for five common vehicles, what changes when you charge in public, the CO₂ you avoid and what the comparison leaves out. To use your own prices and driving, open the [EV vs petrol savings calculator](/tools/ev-vs-petrol-savings-calculator/); it uses the same method and figures.

## How the comparison works

Running cost is the fuel or electricity a vehicle uses for each kilometre, multiplied by the distance you drive. The calculator works it out for a month:

1. **Distance a month** = kilometres a day × driving days.
2. **Fuel cost** = distance ÷ mileage (km a litre, or km a kg for CNG) × fuel price.
3. **Charging cost** = distance ÷ the EV's kilometres per unit × your electricity rate.
4. **Saving** = fuel cost − charging cost, for a month, then × 12 for a year.

The EV figure is kilometres per unit of electricity drawn from the socket or charger, with charging losses included. Use your own vehicle's real figures where you have them, rather than brochure claims, for both the fuel vehicle and the EV.

## The prices used, and when they were checked

Fuel prices change often and differ from city to city, so treat these as a snapshot and type in today's price for your city:

- **Petrol** at ₹102.12 a litre and **diesel** at ₹95.20 a litre, in Delhi on 20 October 2026 ([The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/business/market-compass/petrol-and-diesel-prices-today-in-hyderabad-delhi-chennai-and-mumbai-on-20-october-2026-1134118)).
- **CNG** at ₹86.98 a kg in Delhi, from 29 August 2026 ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/igl-raises-delhi-cng-rates-to-86-98-kg-on-rising-lng-input-costs)).
- **Home charging** at ₹8 a unit: the calculator's assumption for the cost of one extra unit at home in Delhi once the higher slabs and charges apply. Use the rate on your own bill.
- **Public charging** at ₹12 a unit on AC and ₹20 a unit on DC, within the ₹8–15 and ₹15–25 ranges reported by [Statiq, Oct 2026](https://statiq.in/blog/2026/10/12/public-ev-charging-cost-india/).

| Hatchback or sedan, petrol at 15 km a litre (EV: 7 km a unit) | 6.81 | 1.14 | 1.71 | 2.86 |
| SUV, diesel at 14 km a litre (EV: 6 km a unit) | 6.80 | 1.33 | 2.00 | 3.33 |
| Auto-rickshaw, CNG at 30 km a kg (EV: 12 km a unit) | 2.90 | 0.67 | 1.00 | 1.67 |
| Cab, CNG at 22 km a kg (EV: 7 km a unit) | 3.95 | 1.14 | 1.71 | 2.86 |

Even at public DC rates, every vehicle in the table costs less per km on electricity than on fuel. The gap per km is widest for the petrol car and narrowest for a CNG cab that charges at fast chargers.

## Monthly and yearly savings with home charging

| Vehicle | Driving | Fuel a month (₹) | Home charging a month (₹) | Saving a month (₹) | Saving a year (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scooter vs petrol | 30 km a day, 30 days | 2,042 | 240 | 1,802 | 21,629 |
| Hatchback or sedan vs petrol | 40 km a day, 30 days | 8,170 | 1,371 | 6,798 | 81,578 |
| SUV vs diesel | 40 km a day, 30 days | 8,160 | 1,600 | 6,560 | 78,720 |
| Auto-rickshaw vs CNG | 100 km a day, 26 days | 7,538 | 1,733 | 5,805 | 69,659 |
| Cab vs CNG | 150 km a day, 26 days | 15,419 | 4,457 | 10,962 | 1,31,544 |

Amounts are rounded to the rupee, so a saving can differ by ₹1 from fuel minus charging. They match the typical results on the calculator page.

**Scooter or motorcycle.** At 30 km a day, a petrol scooter doing 45 km a litre burns 20 litres a month, ₹2,042 of petrol. An electric scooter doing 30 km a unit uses 30 units, ₹240 at home. Electric scooters are usually charged from a socket at home, so the home rate is the one that counts.

**Hatchback or sedan.** At 40 km a day, a petrol car doing 15 km a litre burns 80 litres a month, ₹8,170 of petrol. An electric car doing 7 km a unit uses about 171 units, ₹1,371 at home. Charge at public AC chargers at ₹12 a unit instead and the saving is ₹6,112 a month; at public DC chargers at ₹20 a unit it is ₹4,741.

**SUV vs diesel.** A diesel SUV doing 14 km a litre spends ₹6.80 a km on diesel, almost exactly what the petrol car spends: diesel costs less a litre, but the SUV goes fewer km on each one. A heavier electric SUV doing 6 km a unit uses 200 units a month at 40 km a day, ₹1,600 at home, so the saving is ₹6,560 a month. Charging only at public DC chargers, the saving is ₹4,160 a month.

**Auto-rickshaw vs CNG.** At 100 km a day for 26 days, a CNG auto doing 30 km a kg uses about 87 kg a month, ₹7,538 of CNG. An electric auto doing 12 km a unit uses about 217 units, ₹1,733 at ₹8 a unit, a saving of ₹5,805 a month.

**Cab vs CNG.** At 150 km a day for 26 days, a CNG cab doing 22 km a kg spends ₹15,419 a month on CNG. An electric cab doing 7 km a unit uses about 557 units: ₹4,457 at ₹8 a unit, a saving of ₹10,962 a month. If it charges only at public DC chargers at ₹20 a unit, its electricity costs ₹11,143 and the saving falls to ₹4,276. For a high-mileage vehicle, where it charges matters almost as much as the switch itself.

## Home charging vs public AC vs public DC

The electricity rate is the input you control most. For the car in our example, at 1,200 km a month:

- **At home at ₹8 a unit:** ₹1,371 a month, or ₹1.14 a km.
- **Public AC charging at ₹12 a unit:** ₹2,057 a month, or ₹1.71 a km.
- **Public DC charging at ₹20 a unit:** ₹3,429 a month, or ₹2.86 a km.

Electricity would have to cost about ₹47.66 a unit before this electric car cost as much per km as the petrol car. For the cab against CNG, the break-even is about ₹27.68 a unit. Both are above the top of the public DC charging range reported today.

Home charging is the least costly, and the safest way to do it is a charger on its own circuit. The central guidelines also let home owners take a separate metered connection with a dedicated EV tariff where their DISCOM offers one ([Lok Sabha answer, Dec 2024](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2985666/1/AS254_WdMGkS.pdf)); ask yours what it would cost per unit. ## The CO₂ you avoid

The calculator counts the tailpipe CO₂ from the fuel you no longer burn, minus the CO₂ from generating the electricity you use instead. For electricity it uses India's grid average of 0.727 kg of CO₂ a unit, from the Central Electricity Authority's CO₂ baseline database for 2023-24 ([SolarQuarter, Jan 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/01/06/co2-baseline-database-highlights-indias-progress-in-renewable-energy-and-emission-reduction-cea/)). For fuel it uses standard tailpipe factors: 2.31 kg a litre of petrol, 2.68 kg a litre of diesel and 2.75 kg a kg of CNG.

For the car at 40 km a day, that is about 2,218 kg of CO₂ a year from petrol against about 1,496 kg from the electricity, so the electric car avoids about 722 kg a year. At the preset distances, the scooter avoids about 293 kg a year, the SUV 1,012 kg, the auto 970 kg and the cab 989 kg.

Charging from [solar panels](/solar-ev-charging/) cuts the electricity side further. Neither side counts the CO₂ from making the vehicle and its battery, or from producing the fuel.

## What this comparison leaves out

Running cost is only part of what a vehicle costs you. Before you decide, add these for the exact models you are considering:

- **Purchase price.** Compare on-road prices after any subsidy or tax benefit in your state. To see how long the running-cost saving takes to cover the difference: months = price difference ÷ monthly saving.
- **Insurance.** Premiums differ by model, insurer and year, so get quotes for both vehicles.
- **Servicing.** An EV has no engine oil to change and fewer moving parts, but tyres, brakes and suspension still wear. Compare the makers' service schedules.
- **Battery life and warranty.** A battery's capacity falls slowly with age and use. Check the years and kilometres the battery warranty covers, and what it says about remaining capacity.
- **Resale value.** It depends on the model, the battery's health and demand when you sell; there is no reliable single figure.
- **A home charger.** If you need a wall box and its installation, add that one-time cost from your own quote.
- **Time and access.** Where and for how long you can charge: at home, at work or on the road.
