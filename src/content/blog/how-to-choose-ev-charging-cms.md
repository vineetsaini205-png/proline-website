The best charge management system (CMS) for an Indian charging network is the one that works with any charger you buy, collects drivers' money into your own account, handles refunds and GST on its own, keeps your data safe, lets you run it in your own brand, and costs the least over five years. Use the 12 checks below on every option you consider, including ours.

## Why the choice matters

A CMS is the software that connects your chargers, lets drivers start and pay, and tells you what is happening at every site. Once chargers, stickers, staff and drivers are set up on one system, changing it is slow and costly. Choosing well at the start saves money and trouble for years.

Many operators compare CMS options on the monthly fee and a demo of the dashboard. The checks that decide whether a CMS is cheap, safe, full-featured and flexible to run are less visible: where the money goes, what happens when a charge fails, who can see your data and what you pay as you grow.

## 1. Does it work with every charger brand you might buy?

Look for **OCPP 1.6J** support for AC, DC and light-EV chargers of any make, not a list of "partner" brands. Ask how a new model is tested before drivers pay on it. Proline CMS runs a 25-check capability test for each model and firmware version, and turns each maker's fault codes into plain words.

Being free to buy any brand is also a cost question: you can choose the charger that suits your budget and site.

## 2. How do drivers pay, and where does the money go?

Drivers in India expect to scan a QR code and pay by UPI, without installing an app or creating an account. Then ask the more important question: **does the money go into your own payment account, or does the platform collect it and pay you later?** When you are the merchant, you are not waiting for payouts, and you are not paying a share of every session.

## 3. Can a driver ever be charged more than they paid?

A safe system bills from the charger's own meter and caps the bill at the amount paid. Unused money should go back automatically, everything should go back if charging never starts, and failed refunds should be retried. These details decide how many angry calls your team gets.

## 4. Does it handle GST properly?

Charging is a taxable service, so every payment needs the right documents: receipt vouchers, tax invoices (B2C, and B2B with the buyer's GSTIN), refund vouchers and credit notes, with CGST and SGST or IGST by place of supply. Ask whether the system issues them automatically and gives your CA a monthly register and a GSTR-1 extract.

## 5. Will the money add up at month end?

Look for a proper ledger and a **daily reconciliation** of payments, refunds and payouts against your payment company and bank. Proline CMS reconciles every morning at 06:00 and opens a ticket for each mismatch, so problems surface in a day, not at the end of the quarter.

## 6. How does it find faults before drivers do?

Uptime is revenue. Check for live status of every gun, alerts with deadlines to acknowledge and fix, tickets with step-by-step checklists, and the ability to take a faulty gun off sale so nobody pays at a broken charger.

## 7. What do your technicians use in the field?

Commissioning and site visits go faster with a guided phone app that works on weak signal, binds each QR sticker to its gun by a real plug-in, and catches swapped or fake stickers. A CMS without field tools leaves your technicians typing server addresses into chargers by hand.

## 8. Where is your data, and who can see it?

Under the DPDP Act 2023, the operator is the Data Fiduciary for drivers' details. Ask where the servers are, how long phone numbers are kept, and when the vendor's staff can see your data. Proline CMS runs on your own server in India or on ours in India, keeps drivers' numbers optional, and lets Proline in only through a time-limited support grant you approve, with every step recorded.

## 9. How secure is sign-in, and who can change what?

Check for sign-in with an authenticator app, roles for each area of work, site limits so people see only their sites, two-person approval for payment settings and large refunds, and an audit log that cannot be quietly edited. See [how Proline CMS keeps drivers, money and data safe](/cms/security/).

## 10. Can you make it yours?

A customisable CMS should carry your name, logo, colours and web address on every screen, receipt and QR sticker, and let you set prices with time-of-day bands, members' prices for societies and fleets, your own roles and alert routing, and connect to your other systems through an API. ## 11. What will it cost over five years?

Charging software is usually sold in one of three ways:

| Model | How you pay | As your network grows |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription | A monthly fee for each charger or connector | The bill grows with every charger |
| Revenue share | A percentage of revenue or a fee per session | The bill grows with every unit sold |
| Owned licence | A one-time licence and a yearly maintenance contract | No fee per charger and no commission |

Add the hidden costs too: staff time for refunds and reconciliation, payouts you wait for, and the cost of switching later. ## 12. What happens if you want to leave?

Ask how you get your data out and how chargers move to another system. With OCPP chargers, moving means pointing each charger to a new address. With Proline CMS on your own server, the database is yours, and reports export to Excel and PDF.
