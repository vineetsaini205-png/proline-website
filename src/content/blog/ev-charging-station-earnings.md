An EV charging station earns the gap between what drivers pay for each unit and what that unit costs you, multiplied by the units you sell, minus fixed charges, rent, commission and running costs. In our worked example below, one 60 kW DC charger in Delhi selling at ₹20 a unit for 3 hours a day earns about ₹43,783 a month after GST and electricity, before rent and staff.

This guide explains each part of that sum with the same method as our [EV charging station earnings calculator](/tools/ev-charging-station-earnings-calculator/), so you can check every number and change it for your own site.

## The earnings formula, step by step

The calculator follows the money for one month:

1. **Units sold** = average power while a vehicle charges × charging hours a day × 30 days × number of chargers.
2. **Revenue** = units sold × the price drivers pay, divided by 1.18 when that price includes 18% GST.
3. **Electricity** = units sold ÷ the charger's efficiency × your EV tariff.
4. **Fixed charges** = your state's fixed charge per kW × the chargers' rated power.
5. **Site and software** = rent, or a revenue share to the landowner, plus any app or aggregator commission.
6. **Other costs** = staff, internet, upkeep and insurance.
7. **Profit** = revenue − electricity − fixed charges − site and software − other costs.

**Average power, not rated power.** A vehicle rarely draws a charger's full power for a whole session: charging slows as the battery fills, and many cars accept less than a fast charger can give. So the calculator assumes a 60 kW DC charger delivers 40 kW on average, and a 7.4 kW AC charger 6.5 kW.

**GST comes out first.** Public charging is a service taxed at 18% GST ([Business Today, Sep 2024](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/policy/story/no-exemption-from-gst-on-electric-vehicle-charging-at-public-charging-stations-444319-2024-09-03)), and the price drivers see usually includes it. That GST goes to the government, so ₹20 a unit leaves you ₹16.95 of revenue.

**You buy more units than you sell.** Some energy turns into heat inside the charger. The calculator assumes DC chargers are 94% efficient and AC chargers 97%, so selling 3,600 units from a DC charger means buying about 3,830 from your DISCOM.

## Utilisation: the number that decides your profit

Utilisation is how many hours a day each charger is actually charging a vehicle. It matters more than any other input, because revenue rises with every hour of use while rent, fixed charges and upkeep stay the same. The calculator offers three cases, in hours of charging a day per charger:

- **7.4 kW AC:** quiet 2 hours, likely 4, busy 6.
- **60 kW DC:** quiet 1.5 hours, likely 3, busy 5.

Three hours is one-eighth of the day, so even the "likely" DC case leaves the charger free most of the time. In our Delhi example, profit moves from about ₹21,891 a month when quiet to ₹43,783 when likely and ₹72,972 when busy. Our guide to [starting an EV charging station](/start-an-ev-charging-station/) shows how to judge the demand at a site.

## Expect 6–12 months to build up

A new station is rarely busy from day one: drivers have to find it on maps and apps. Bolt.Earth notes that utilisation typically ramps up over 6–12 months ([Bolt.Earth](https://bolt.earth/franchise)). Plan your cash flow on the "quiet" result for the first months. A fixed rent hurts most in this period, while a revenue share costs less when the station is quiet and more when it is busy.

## State EV tariffs and fixed charges

Your electricity cost depends on your state's EV tariff, set by its electricity regulatory commission. The table shows the LT tariffs for public charging stations in the states we work in, and what the same 60 kW DC charger earns in each at 3 hours a day and ₹20 a unit.

| State | EV tariff (₹ a unit) | Fixed charge (₹ a month) | Example profit a month (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delhi | 4.50 | None | 43,783 |
| Chhattisgarh | 5.00 | None | 41,868 |
| Himachal Pradesh | 5.82 | None | 38,728 |
| Uttarakhand | 6.25 | None | 37,081 |
| Tamil Nadu | 6 to 9, by time of day | 25 to 138 per kVA | 36,538 (lowest rates) |
| Haryana | 6.62 | None | 35,664 |
| Telangana | 6.00 | 50 per kW | 35,038 |
| Madhya Pradesh | 6.79 | None | 35,013 |
| Maharashtra | 6.08 | 75 per kW | 33,232 |
| Uttar Pradesh | 7.70 | None | 31,528 |

Tariffs from the [BEE state-wise EV tariff list](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/annexure-A.pdf). HT connections and some DISCOMs differ, and tariff orders change, so use the rate on your own bill or your DISCOM's latest tariff order. The calculator works out fixed charges on the chargers' rated power; your bill uses your sanctioned or contract load, which can be higher.

## The time-of-day rule

The Ministry of Power's 2024 guidelines suggest a single-part tariff for public charging, capped at the average cost of supply until 31 March 2028, 30% cheaper in solar hours and 30% dearer in other hours ([Lok Sabha answer, Aug 2025](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2994845/1/AU3099_jxi0H3.pdf)). Your state regulator decides how it applies, in its tariff order.

The calculator turns the rule into a factor on your tariff: 0.7 × the share of units sold in solar hours, plus 1.3 × the rest. At exactly half, the factor is 1 and you pay what you would on a flat tariff. Sell more of your units in solar hours, as a workplace might, and the bill falls; charge a fleet overnight and it rises.

In our Delhi example, with 40% of units in solar hours, electricity rises from ₹17,234 to ₹18,268 a month and profit falls to ₹42,749. With 70%, electricity falls to ₹15,166 and profit rises to ₹45,851.

## Worked example 1: one 60 kW DC charger in Delhi

These are the calculator's default settings, so you can open it and see the same result.

- **Assumptions:** one 60 kW DC charger, 3 hours of charging a day, drivers pay ₹20 a unit including GST (the middle of the ₹15–25 range for public DC charging reported by [Statiq, Oct 2026](https://statiq.in/blog/2026/10/12/public-ev-charging-cost-india/)), Delhi EV tariff of ₹4.50 a unit with no fixed charge, and no rent, commission or other costs.
- **Units sold:** 40 kW × 3 hours × 30 days = 3,600 kWh.
- **Revenue after GST:** 3,600 × ₹20 ÷ 1.18 = ₹61,017. The other ₹10,983 that drivers pay is GST.
- **Electricity:** 3,600 ÷ 0.94 × ₹4.50 = ₹17,234 (you buy about 3,830 units to sell 3,600).
- **Profit:** ₹61,017 − ₹17,234 = ₹43,783 a month, or about ₹5,25,395 a year.

The default leaves rent, commission and other costs at zero. Every 10% of revenue you pay away, as a revenue share or an app commission, costs ₹6,102 a month here: with 10% to the landowner and 10% to an app (example figures, not market rates), profit falls to ₹31,580. Rent and other costs come off the profit rupee for rupee, and every ₹1 a unit you change your price moves revenue by about ₹3,051 a month.

## Worked example 2: two 7.4 kW AC chargers at a hotel

Say a hotel in Maharashtra, picked to show how a fixed charge works, puts two 7.4 kW AC chargers in its car park on their own meter at the LT EV tariff. Under the 2024 guidelines, a DISCOM may instead provide a sub-meter behind a hotel's existing HT connection ([Mercom, Sep 2024](https://www.mercomindia.com/ministry-of-power-releases-ev-charging-infrastructure-guidelines)).

- **Assumptions:** two 7.4 kW AC chargers, 4 hours of charging a day each, drivers pay ₹12 a unit including GST (within the ₹8–15 range Statiq reports for public AC charging), Maharashtra EV tariff of ₹6.08 a unit plus ₹75 per kW a month fixed, and no rent or commission.
- **Units sold:** 6.5 kW × 4 hours × 30 days × 2 chargers = 1,560 kWh.
- **Revenue after GST:** 1,560 × ₹12 ÷ 1.18 = ₹15,864.
- **Electricity:** 1,560 ÷ 0.97 × ₹6.08 = ₹9,778.
- **Fixed charges:** ₹75 × 7.4 kW × 2 chargers = ₹1,110 a month on the electricity bill.
- **Profit:** ₹15,864 − ₹9,778 − ₹1,110 = ₹4,976 a month.

At the quiet 2 hours a day the profit is about ₹1,933 a month, and at the busy 6 hours about ₹8,019. The same two chargers in Delhi, at ₹4.50 a unit with no fixed charge, would make ₹8,627 a month at 4 hours a day.

AC charging keeps a thin margin: each unit sold here brings ₹10.17 of revenue after GST and costs ₹6.27 in electricity, leaving ₹3.90 before the fixed charge, against ₹12.16 a unit for the DC charger in Delhi. For a hotel, the chargers may also win guests who need to charge overnight, which the calculator does not count. If the chargers run on the hotel's existing commercial connection without a separate EV meter, use that tariff instead.

## Work out payback with your own quote

We don't publish what a station costs, because the chargers, connection, cabling, earthing and civil work change from site to site. Once you have a quote, payback is a simple division:

**Payback in months = your all-in quote ÷ monthly profit.**

- **Use an all-in quote:** chargers, connection, panels, cabling, earthing, civil work and software.
- **Allow for the ramp-up.** For a cautious answer, count the first 12 months at the quiet profit and the rest at the likely profit: payback in months = 12 + (your quote − 12 × quiet profit) ÷ likely profit, when your quote is more than a year of quiet profit.
- **Look five years ahead:** cash after 5 years = monthly profit × 60 − your quote.

Enter your quote in the [earnings calculator](/tools/ev-charging-station-earnings-calculator/) to see both; we never fill the quote in for you. It leaves out loan interest, depreciation, income tax, charger downtime and future price changes, so add those with your accountant.
