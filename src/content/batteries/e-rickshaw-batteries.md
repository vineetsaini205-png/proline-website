## Lead-acid or lithium: how they compare

Most e-rickshaws in India started on lead-acid: four 12 V batteries in series, making 48 V. More drivers and fleets now choose lithium iron phosphate (LFP) packs, because an e-rickshaw runs long hours every day, and that is where lithium's longer life pays back.

| | Lead-acid (4 × 12 V) | Lithium LFP (51.2 V) |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Heavy: four batteries, often well over 100 kg | About a third of the weight, or less |
| Usable capacity | About half, to keep the batteries healthy | 80–100% |
| Life | 500–1,000 cycles on paper; often replaced after about a year of daily running | 2,000 cycles or more on most makers' datasheets |
| Charging time | 6–10 hours, usually overnight | 2–4 hours, depending on the charger's current |
| Maintenance | Water top-ups for flooded types, clean terminals | No top-ups; clean connectors and the right charger |
| In summer heat | Life falls sharply | Holds up better; the BMS cuts off if it overheats |
| Cost to buy | Lower | Higher |
| Cost over its life | Higher for daily use: more replacements and more hours off the road | Lower for an e-rickshaw that runs every day |

The figures are typical ([EMobility+](https://emobilityplus.com/2021/09/01/understanding-evolution-of-e-rickshaws-market-in-india-valve-regulated-lead-acid-vrla-to-lithium-ion-battery/amp/), [WRI India](https://wri-india.org/blog/powering-indian-e-rickshaws-toward-alternate-battery-technologies)); the datasheet of the exact battery is what counts.

**Compare the energy each battery delivers over its life, not just what it costs to buy.** Multiply the usable kWh by the cycles the maker states, then divide each quote by that number. For example:

- A 51.2 V, 100 Ah LFP pack, used to 90% for 2,000 cycles: 5.12 × 0.9 × 2,000 ≈ 9,200 kWh over its life.
- A 48 V, 140 Ah lead-acid set, used to half for 500 cycles: 6.72 × 0.5 × 500 ≈ 1,700 kWh.

The lithium pack delivers more than five times the energy, so the lead-acid set has to cost far less to come out ahead.

## Voltage: 48 V, 51.2 V and 60 V

- **48 V lead-acid:** four 12 V batteries in series, the most common e-rickshaw system.
- **51.2 V lithium:** 16 LFP cells of 3.2 V in series. This is the usual lithium replacement for a 48 V lead-acid set, and its voltage stays close to that of a charged lead-acid set, so most 48 V e-rickshaws run on it. Check your controller's voltage range all the same.
- **60 V:** some e-rickshaws, e-carts and loaders use five 12 V lead-acid batteries, or a 60 V-class lithium pack. Lithium packs sold as "60 V" are built with different numbers of cells, so match the charger to the pack's actual cell count and full-charge voltage, not to the label.

## How many Ah you need

Lead-acid e-rickshaw sets commonly come in about 100 to 150 Ah, and lithium packs in about 80 to 150 Ah. Because almost all of a lithium pack's capacity is usable, fewer Ah do the same work: a 51.2 V, 100 Ah LFP pack (5.1 kWh, about 4.6 kWh usable) gives more usable energy than a 48 V, 140 Ah lead-acid set used to half (about 3.4 kWh).

To size a pack for your route:

1. **Find your Wh per km.** Divide the battery's usable Wh by the kilometres your e-rickshaw runs on a full charge.
2. **Multiply by your daily km.**
3. **Add about 20%** for ageing, heat and full loads of passengers.

Say your e-rickshaw uses 45 Wh per km and runs 100 km a day: that is 4.5 kWh, or about 5.4 kWh with the margin, which a 51.2 V, 105 Ah pack covers. A smaller pack with a top-up charge at lunch also works, if you have a charging point where you stop.

## Match the charger, controller and BMS

- **Change the charger with the battery.** A lithium charger charges at a set current, then holds the final voltage (about 58.4 V for a 16-cell LFP pack) and stops. A lead-acid charger works differently, and its boost or equalising stage can push a lithium pack past its limit.
- **Choose the charging current.** A 20 A charger fills a 100 Ah pack from empty in about five hours, a 30 A charger in about three and a half. Faster charging needs a pack and BMS made for that current, and a socket and circuit that can supply it.
- **Check the BMS current.** With a full load or on a slope, the motor draws a high current. The BMS's continuous and peak current limits must be above what your controller draws, or the pack will cut out when you need it most.
- **Fit it securely.** The pack should sit firmly in its tray, protected from splashes, with cables that can't rub through on rough roads.

Lithium e-rickshaw batteries have safety rules too. ICAT's list of type-approval tests for e-rickshaws and e-carts includes the battery pack test under AIS-156 Part 2, Amendment 3 ([ICAT](https://icat.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/E-cart-E-rickshaw.pdf)), the same battery safety standard that covers electric scooters (see [2-wheeler batteries](/batteries/2-wheeler-batteries/)). When you buy a lithium pack, ask for its test report and the BMS specifications.

## Charging hubs, swapping and fleets

- **Charging at a depot or hub.** Many e-rickshaws charge overnight at a depot or hub, each on its own socket point and charger. Each point needs its own protection (an MCB and an RCD) and a sound earth, the cabling must be sized for all the chargers running together through the night, and the sanctioned load must cover them. See [2W and 3W chargers](/chargers/2w-3w-chargers/).
- **Battery swapping.** At a swap station, a driver exchanges an empty pack for a charged one in a few minutes, and the network owns the batteries. You pay per swap or for a plan, the e-rickshaw must take the network's packs, and you need a station on your route.
- **Fleets.** With ten or more e-rickshaws, standardise on one pack and charger model, plan charging around shifts, and keep a record of each pack's cycles and faults, so you spot ageing packs early. See [fleets](/for/fleets/).

Hand old batteries back to the seller or a recycler. Under the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022, producers must collect waste batteries and send them for recycling or refurbishment ([Autocar Pro, Aug 2022](https://autocarpro.in/news-national/centre-notifies-new-battery-waste-management-norms-92626)).
