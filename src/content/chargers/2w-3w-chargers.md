## What an e-rickshaw charger does

Most e-rickshaws don't plug into a charging station. Each one has an off-board charger, a box that turns mains AC into the DC its battery needs, plugged into a wall socket. So there are two things to get right:

- **The battery charger.** It must match the pack: 48 V or 60 V, lead-acid or lithium.
- **The charging point.** A 16 A socket on its own protected circuit, or a metered multi-point charger at a hub where many vehicles charge.

**Lead-acid packs,** usually four or five 12 V batteries in series for 48 or 60 V, charge slowly, often overnight. They give off hydrogen as they charge, so they need a well-ventilated place, not a closed room.

**Lithium packs** charge faster and need a charger made for their chemistry, set to the pack's full-charge voltage and the charging profile its battery management system (BMS) expects. When an owner moves from lead-acid to lithium, the charger must change too; see [e-rickshaw batteries](/batteries/e-rickshaw-batteries/).

| Vehicle | How it usually charges | What to install |
|---|---|---|
| E-rickshaw or e-cart, 48 or 60 V | Off-board charger on a wall socket | Socket points, or a multi-point charger at a hub |
| E-scooter or e-bike | Portable charger on a socket; some models also take DC fast charging | Socket points, plus Type 6 or Type 7 chargers for scooters that support them |
| E-auto or cargo 3-wheeler | Off-board charger, or DC fast charging on models that support it | Socket points, plus Type 6 or Type 7 DC chargers |

## Socket points for portable chargers

A charger that draws current for hours needs more than a spare socket or an extension board. A safe charging point has:

- **A 16 A socket on its own circuit,** with an MCB and an RCD, and one socket for each vehicle.
- **The right socket for the chargers' plugs,** with a weatherproof cover at outdoor points.
- **A sound earth,** tested for resistance.
- **Sockets mounted high** enough that vehicles can't hit them and rainwater can't reach them, with cables kept off the floor.
- **A parking spot beside each socket,** so charging cables don't cross walkways.
- **A fire extinguisher suited to electrical fires** near the charging area.

## Multi-point chargers for e-rickshaw hubs and parking

A multi-point charger is one cabinet or pillar with several charging outputs, each with its own protection and switching, and often its own energy meter. It suits e-rickshaw hubs, parking contractors, fleet yards and markets where many vehicles charge at once.

- **Billing for each vehicle.** Drivers start a session with an RFID card, a QR code or an app, and pay for the energy or time they use.
- **One view of every point.** Chargers that speak OCPP 1.6J can run on a charge management system (CMS) that shows every session and fault. [Proline CMS](/cms/) works with light-EV chargers of any make that speak OCPP 1.6J.
- **Balanced load.** On a three-phase supply, the outputs are spread across the phases so no single phase is overloaded.

Size the hub for its busiest hours. Add up the input rating on each charger's label, leave headroom for lights and fans, and check that your sanctioned load covers the total. For a hub that sells charging, ask your DISCOM about a separate connection on the EV tariff.

## Type 6 and Type 7 chargers for light EVs

India has its own charging connectors for scooters, 3-wheelers and other light EVs, in IS 17017, India's standard for EV charging ([EVreporter, Apr 2024](https://evreporter.com/towards-standardization-of-dc-charging-for-light-e-vehicles/)):

- **Type 6 (IS 17017-2-6)** is a DC connector for light EVs, for up to 120 V and about 100 A.
- **Type 7 (IS 17017-2-7),** also called LECCS, takes both AC charging and DC fast charging through one compact inlet.

Only vehicles with the matching inlet can use these chargers, and many e-rickshaws and older scooters still charge from a portable charger on a socket. So list the vehicles that will use your site, and their inlets, before you buy; a public point often needs socket points as well. We compare chargers from the brands we work with for that mix.

## Battery swapping

Some scooter and 3-wheeler fleets swap batteries instead of charging them: the driver exchanges an empty pack for a charged one at a swap station in a few minutes, and the swap network charges the packs in racks. Delhi's EV Policy 2026 lets both plug-in and battery-swapping 2-wheelers and 3-wheelers qualify for its incentives ([Daily Pioneer, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.dailypioneer.com/news/slug-lite/delhi-notifies-ev-policy-2026-subsidies-within-60-days?year=2026)). If you host a swap station, its racks need a supply, a DB box and earthing like any charger.

## Charging points at dealerships in Delhi

Under the Delhi EV Policy 2026, every vehicle maker operating in Delhi must set up at least one public charging station at each of its dealers, with at least three charging points for 2-wheelers and 3-wheelers and two for cars ([Daily Pioneer, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.dailypioneer.com/news/slug-lite/delhi-notifies-ev-policy-2026-subsidies-within-60-days?year=2026)). For a 2W or 3W dealer, the points can be socket points, Type 6 or Type 7 chargers, or a mix, to suit the models it sells. As the station is public, plan how drivers start and pay, such as QR payment on OCPP chargers. We help makers and dealers choose the points and install them; see [EV makers and dealers](/for/ev-makers-and-dealers/).

## What the installation needs

- **Protection for each point:** an MCB and the RCD type the charger maker asks for, with surge protection, in a DB box. See [EV charger DB boxes](/services/ev-charger-db-box/).
- **Cable sized for continuous current,** armoured where it runs underground.
- **A tested earth** for every installation.
- **Enough sanctioned load,** spread across the phases at a hub.
- **A shed or canopy** where vehicles charge in the open, with lighting for night use.
- **Ventilation** wherever lead-acid packs charge.
