## What a white-label charger is

A white-label charger is designed, built and tested by a charger maker, then sold under another company's brand. The electronics, safety design and software are the maker's, proven on its own model. The name, the colours and the look are yours, so your customers see one brand from the vehicle or the app to the charger on the wall.

It puts your name on proven chargers without designing your own. The trade-off is choice: you start from what the maker already builds, and deeper changes depend on the maker and the size of your order.

## Who white-label chargers suit

- **New EV brands** that want a home charger in their own brand, delivered with the vehicle. See [EV makers and dealers](/for/ev-makers-and-dealers/).
- **Charge point operators** that want one look at every site, so drivers recognise the network from the road. See [charge point operators](/for/charge-point-operators/).
- **Real-estate developers** that want chargers in the project's brand in housing societies, offices and malls.
- **Dealers and distributors** that want to sell chargers under their own name, alongside vehicles or electrical goods.

If you need only a handful of chargers, or your customers care little whose name is on them, a standard model from the brands we work with is simpler. We tell you which way fits.

## Choosing the base model

White-label chargers can be AC wall boxes or DC fast chargers. Start from your customers and their vehicles, not from a catalogue:

- **A home charger sold with your vehicles:** an AC wall box matched to the vehicles' onboard charger, often 7.4 kW for cars.
- **A public network:** DC fast chargers sized for each site, with OCPP 1.6J, a screen and QR payment, plus AC chargers where cars park for hours.
- **Housing societies and offices:** AC chargers with RFID cards and OCPP, so residents or staff can be billed.
- **A record in India:** a model already running in Indian heat and dust, with spares and service in the states where your chargers will go.

## What you can brand, and what stays the maker's

What you can usually brand:

- **The body:** your logo and colours, as a printed front panel, decals or a body colour, depending on the model.
- **The screen:** the start-up screen, welcome messages and colours, on chargers with a display.
- **The app name:** where the maker offers a branded version of its app.
- **QR stickers and labels** for payment and support, with your name and helpline.
- **Packaging, manuals and warranty cards.**

What stays the maker's: the internal design, the safety electronics and the core of the firmware. Ask what has to stay on the rating label, and whose name appears on the test reports.

## Questions to ask before you order

- **Test reports and certificates for the base model.** Which lab tested it, such as ARAI or ICAT, against which standard, such as IS 17017, India's standard for EV charging, and how the reports apply to your branded version.
- **Warranty and spares.** Who honours the warranty, you or the maker, what it covers, and whether spares stay available for as long as your customers will use the charger.
- **Firmware updates.** Who delivers them, whether over the air or on site, and whether your branding survives each update.
- **OCPP.** Whether the base model supports OCPP 1.6J, so it can run on your chosen CMS, and whether it works without the maker's cloud.
- **Minimum order and lead time.** The smallest order the maker accepts for each branding option, and how long production takes. Lead times depend on the maker and the size of your order.
- **Who else sells it.** Whether the same base model is sold under other brands in your market.
- **Who supports your customers.** Whose number is on the sticker, and how faults reach the maker.
