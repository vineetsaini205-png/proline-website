## What businesses ask before adding EV charging

Whether you run a hotel, a mall, a hospital, an office, a fuel station, a dhaba or a shop, the first questions tend to be the same:

- Which chargers do we need, AC or DC, and how many?
- Will they earn money, or only add to our electricity bill?
- Is our electricity connection enough?
- Is there a subsidy?
- Who takes payment from drivers, and how?

The answers depend on how long vehicles stay at your site and what your supply can take.

## Match the charger to how long vehicles stay

The length of the stop decides the charger. A car parked overnight needs only a few kilowatts; a car stopping for twenty minutes needs a DC fast charger.

| Business | How long EVs stay | What usually fits |
|---|---|---|
| Hotel or resort | Overnight | 7.4 or 11 kW AC at guest bays; DC only on highways |
| Office or factory campus | The working day | 7.4 kW AC for staff, with some 11 kW points |
| Hospital | Staff shifts, and visits of one to three hours | AC for staff and visitors; a DC charger for taxis if the load allows |
| Mall, cinema or market | One to three hours | AC chargers, plus a DC charger for quick top-ups |
| Restaurant, cafe or shop | Under an hour | A 30 or 60 kW DC charger, or AC where customers stay longer |
| Fuel station or highway dhaba | A short break or a meal | DC chargers of 60 kW and up, often with two guns |

AC chargers need far less supply than DC, and the car sets their speed: many cars sold in India accept 7.2 kW on AC and some 11 kW, so a 22 kW AC charger adds nothing for them. DC chargers of 30–60 kW run on a three-phase LT connection, while 120 kW and above may need HT supply. See [DC fast chargers](/chargers/dc-fast-chargers/).

## Free perk or paid service

Decide this early, because it changes the chargers and the software.

- **Free for guests or staff.** The simplest set-up. RFID cards or an app login keep outsiders off the chargers, and chargers with a built-in meter record the energy each session uses.
- **Paid charging.** You set a price per unit and drivers pay. That needs networked chargers that support OCPP, and software: an app platform that takes a commission, or your own charge management system (CMS).
- **Hosting a charging network.** Some charging networks put their own chargers at host sites and pay rent or a share of revenue. We install for operators too; see [charge point operators](/for/charge-point-operators/).

[Proline CMS](/cms/) is our own charge management software. Your brand goes on the QR codes and receipts, drivers pay by UPI into your own account, unused money is refunded automatically and GST documents are issued for you. Staff can get their own price, or chargers only they can use. There is no commission on your charging payments, and you run it on your own server or we host it on servers in India, with 24/7 support for the CMS.

## What a charger could earn

A paid charger earns the gap between what drivers pay for each unit and what that unit costs you, times the units you sell. Rent, software and upkeep come out of that gap.

- **What drivers pay:** about ₹8–15 per kWh at public AC chargers and ₹15–25 per kWh at DC chargers ([Statiq, Oct 2026](https://statiq.in/blog/2026/10/12/public-ev-charging-cost-india/)).
- **What a unit costs you:** the LT EV tariff in the states we work in runs from ₹4.50 a unit in Delhi to ₹7.70 in Uttar Pradesh, and some states add a fixed charge for every kW of load ([BEE state-wise EV tariffs](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/annexure-A.pdf)).
- **When cars charge:** under the Ministry of Power's 2024 guidelines, where time-of-day rates apply, energy in solar hours costs 0.7 times the tariff and other hours 1.3 times ([Lok Sabha answer, Aug 2025](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2994845/1/AU3099_jxi0H3.pdf)). Offices and malls, where cars charge in the daytime, gain from this.

What decides profit is utilisation: how many hours a day vehicles actually charge. Usage at a new station typically builds over 6–12 months ([Bolt.Earth](https://bolt.earth/franchise)), so plan for quiet early months.

## Power supply and permissions

Your electricity connection shapes the plan as much as the chargers do.

- **Spare load.** One or two AC chargers often fit within your existing sanctioned load; we check it from your bill.
- **Separate metering.** The Ministry of Power's 2024 guidelines call for separate metering at each charging station, and let DISCOMs provide a sub-meter behind an existing HT connection, which helps hotels, malls and hospitals that have one ([guidelines, Sep 2024](https://beeindia.gov.in/WriteReadData/L45218/2841453721548450.pdf)).
- **LT or HT.** The same guidelines provide for LT connections of up to 150 kW for charging stations, though states apply this differently. Above that, you need HT supply with its own transformer, which adds cost, space and time.
- **Fuel stations.** The guidelines' list of documents for a charging connection includes approval from PESO, the Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation, for chargers at petrol pumps.
- **Basements.** The same list includes a fire department NOC for a charger in a basement, which matters for malls and hospitals with underground parking.

The connection is taken in your name; we tell you the load to apply for and the documents to keep ready.

## Subsidies: check, but don't count on one

The central PM E-DRIVE scheme supports public charging, but it is not an automatic grant. Proposals go through government nodal agencies, and the support is approval-based ([ChargeZone](https://www.chargezone.co.in/franchise)). The scheme names site types and support levels: up to 100% at government premises that are open free to the public, and support on upstream infrastructure, the power supply side of a station, at sites such as shopping malls, markets and highway roadside facilities ([AIR News, Sep 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/centre-issues-guidelines-for-rollout-of-72300-ev-charging-stations-under-pm-e-drive/)).

Build the plan to pay its way without a subsidy, and treat any support as a bonus. We tell you whether your site type appears in PM E-DRIVE or your state's EV policy.
