## What turnkey station setup covers

A charging station brings several trades together: electrical, civil and software work, plus the electricity connection from your DISCOM. We plan them as one job, so trenches are dug once and every charger has its foundation, cable and software ready when it arrives.

| Stage | What we do | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Site and power check | Survey the supply, load, cable routes, earth, space and signal | Give access and the electricity bill |
| Chargers | Compare brands for your site, then supply them or install yours | Choose the brand and how many |
| Permissions | List what your site needs and the documents to keep ready | Apply for the connection, get the NOCs |
| Build | Foundations, trenches, canopy, panels, cabling and earthing | Agree the layout |
| Branding | Plan sign positions, fixings and power supply | Choose the signs, share your logo |
| Software | Connect every charger to Proline CMS, your CMS or a network's app | Choose the software |
| Go-live | Tests, test charges, handover and help with listings | Open the station to drivers |

## Site and power check

Every station starts with a [site survey](/services/site-survey/). An engineer checks your connection and sanctioned load, whether LT supply can carry the chargers you want, the route and distance to each charger, the existing earth, the space for panels and bays, and the mobile signal the chargers need to reach their software. The survey is paid, and the fee is adjusted against your order if you go ahead.

The connection shapes the whole design. The central guidelines let charging stations opt for an LT connection for loads up to 150 kW ([Lok Sabha answer, December 2024](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2985666/1/AS254_WdMGkS.pdf)), though what your DISCOM offers depends on your state's rules. Above that, the site needs an HT connection with its own transformer and HT panel, which adds cost, space and time.

## Choosing chargers, any brand

We don't make chargers, so we have no single brand to push. We compare chargers from the many brands we work with on power, number of guns, connectors, OCPP support, warranty and service in your state. The advice is free, and we install chargers you buy elsewhere too.

The mix follows how long vehicles stop: DC fast chargers for highways and cab hubs, AC chargers where cars park for hours, light-EV chargers for e-scooter and e-rickshaw hubs. Size the trench, panel and canopy for the chargers you plan to add, not just the first ones. See [DC fast chargers](/chargers/dc-fast-chargers/).

## Permissions checklist

Which permissions a station needs depends on the site. After the survey we list the ones yours needs and tell you which documents to keep ready. The usual ones:

- **No electricity licence.** Setting up and running a charging station is a de-licensed activity under the Ministry of Power's 2024 guidelines ([Mercom, Sep 2024](https://www.mercomindia.com/ministry-of-power-releases-ev-charging-infrastructure-guidelines)).
- **The electricity connection.** A new connection, a load increase or a separate meter from your DISCOM, in the site owner's name, on your state's EV tariff.
- **Electrical inspection.** Under the CEA's 2023 safety regulations, an installation above the voltage your state government has notified must be inspected by the Electrical Inspector before supply starts; at or below it, the installation goes through self-certification ([CEA safety regulations, 2023](https://cea.nic.in/regulations-category/measures-relating-to-safety-and-electric-supply/?lang=en), regulation 45). Your DISCOM or the state's electrical inspectorate can tell you which applies.
- **The land.** Ownership papers, or a lease or revenue-share agreement in writing that lets you lay cables, build a canopy and put up signs.
- **The property owner's consent.** A NOC from the housing society, mall, office building or landlord when the station sits on their property.
- **Local permissions.** Check with your local body whether a canopy or a large sign needs its approval, and get permission from the road's owner before a cable crosses a public road or footpath.

## Civil work and canopy

Civil work comes first, because foundations, trenches and cable-entry pipes have to be ready before the chargers arrive.

- **Plinths** for DC chargers and pedestals, raised above the site's flood level, with anchor bolts set to the maker's template.
- **Trenches and pipes** for every cable run, with sleeves under driveways and spare pipes for future chargers.
- **Bays:** paving or levelling, bollards or wheel stops, lighting and floor marking.
- **A canopy** over the chargers and bays, plain or with solar panels on top. See [civil work](/services/ev-charging-civil-work/) and [solar EV charging](/solar-ev-charging/).

## Cabling, panels and earthing

From the meter or transformer, we build the electrical path to every charger:

- **An LT panel** with an incomer, a breaker for each charger, surge protection and, if you want it, a meter for each charger.
- **Armoured cable** sized for each charger's input current and the length of its run, in trenches or on cable tray. See [cabling](/services/ev-charger-cabling/).
- **DB boxes** for AC chargers, with the MCB, RCD and surge protection each maker specifies.
- **Dedicated earth pits,** tested for resistance, with the values recorded.

All of it follows the charger makers' manuals, the CEA safety regulations and the Indian Standards that apply, such as IS 17017 for EV charging, IS 732 for wiring and IS 3043 for earthing.

## Branding and signage

Drivers have to find your station and trust it, so plan your brand where they look:

- a sign visible from the road, and direction signs inside a large car park
- your name and colours on the canopy fascia
- EV-only bay signs and floor marking, to keep petrol and diesel cars out of the bays
- clear instructions at each charger: how to start a charge, how to pay and the number to call
- your brand on the chargers themselves, with [white-label chargers](/chargers/white-label-chargers/)

We plan the foundations, fixings and power supply for signs and a lit fascia along with the civil and electrical work, so nothing has to be dug up again when they go up.

## Software: Proline CMS, your own CMS or a network app

Every public charger needs software to start sessions, take payment and tell you when a charger goes offline. We connect your chargers to whichever you choose:

- **[Proline CMS](/cms/),** our own charge management software. It runs chargers of any brand over OCPP 1.6J, in your brand, on your own server or hosted by us in India. Drivers scan a QR code on the gun and pay by UPI into your own account, with no commission on charging payments and no fee per charger per month.
- **Your own CMS.** We set each charger's backend URL, charge point ID and security settings, and check that sessions reach your system.
- **A charging network's app platform,** if you join a network or franchise, connected the way the network asks.

Buy chargers that support OCPP, so you can change software later without changing chargers.

## Commissioning and go-live

Before the station opens, every charger is commissioned: electrical tests, network and firmware settings, and a test charge on each gun that shows up in your software. You get a handover pack with the results, photos and settings, and your site staff get a short walk-through on using the chargers safely. The full checklist is on our [commissioning](/services/ev-charger-commissioning/) page.

Then drivers need to find you:

- **Google Maps.** A Google Business Profile for the station puts it on the map with its exact location, opening hours and photos.
- **EV Yatra.** The Bureau of Energy Efficiency keeps a national online database of public charging stations: operators register their stations on its EV Yatra portal, and its app helps drivers find the nearest public charger ([Power Line, Dec 2022](https://powerline.net.in/2022/12/14/ev-yatra-web-portal-and-mobile-app-launched/)). Proline CMS prepares the station and energy files for it.
- **Unified Bharat eCharge (UBC).** The government's national charging network, launched in August 2026: drivers find chargers and pay by UPI in the BHIM app, while operators keep their own chargers and brand ([Autocar Professional, Aug 2026](https://www.autocarpro.in/news/pulse-energy-joins-unified-bharat-echarge-as-certified-tech-enabler-134136)). Stations on [Proline CMS](/cms/built-for-india/) can join in a few simple steps, now or later.
- **Apps and fleets.** On a network's platform, your chargers appear in its app. Tie-ups with cab, delivery and e-rickshaw fleets nearby can bring regular users early.

We help with each listing: the exact location, charger types, connectors, power and photos. Expect quiet early months, as usage at a new station typically builds over 6–12 months ([Bolt.Earth](https://bolt.earth/franchise)).
