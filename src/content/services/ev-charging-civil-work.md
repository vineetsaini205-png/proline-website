## What our civil work covers

- **Foundations:** concrete plinths for DC chargers and AC pedestals, with anchor bolts and cable entries cast in.
- **Trenches and ducts:** cable trenches, pipes for future cables, draw pits and road crossings, with the surface made good.
- **Protection:** bollards, wheel stops, kerbs or a raised island, so vehicles can't hit the chargers.
- **Canopies:** steel structures with a sheet roof or solar panels, over the chargers and the bays.
- **Marking, signs and lighting:** bay marking, EV-only and charger-type signs, and lighting for use at night.
- **Drainage:** levels, slopes and drains that keep water away from chargers, cable entries and pits.

## Charger foundations and plinths

A floor-standing DC charger can weigh several hundred kilograms and takes its heavy supply cable from below, so it stands on a concrete plinth. The charger maker's foundation drawing gives the plinth's size, the bolt pattern and where the cable enters, and we build to it:

- **A PCC levelling bed, then an RCC plinth,** with the reinforcement and concrete grade the drawing or the site design calls for.
- **Anchor bolts set with the maker's template,** so they line up with the holes in the charger's base. Where the maker allows, chemical anchors can be drilled after the concrete has set.
- **Cable-entry pipes cast in,** with bends wide enough to pull heavy DC cable through.
- **A plinth raised above the highest water level the site has seen,** so water never reaches the cable entry at the base of the cabinet.
- **Space around the cabinet** for airflow and for opening its doors, as the maker asks.
- **Concrete cured properly** before the charger is bolted down.

AC pedestal chargers need a smaller foundation with a cable entry. Wall-mounted chargers need no foundation, only a sound wall at the right spot.

## Cable trenches, ducts and road crossings

- **The route** is agreed at the [site survey](/services/site-survey/): as short as practical, clear of water lines, drains and other services, and dug to the depth the design calls for.
- **Armoured cable** is laid on a sand bed and covered with protective bricks or tiles, with warning tape above. Where cables may need to be pulled out later, they run in HDPE or DWC pipe.
- **Draw pits** with covers go at sharp bends and on long runs, so cable can be pulled and inspected without digging.
- **Driveways and internal roads** are crossed in GI or HDPE pipe, encased in concrete where heavy vehicles pass.
- **Duct ends are sealed,** so water and rodents can't travel along them into a charger or a DB box.
- **The surface is made good:** concrete, tiles, paver blocks or tar.

Crossing a public road or footpath needs permission from the authority that owns it; we tell you what to ask for. If you plan more chargers, lay spare ducts now: it saves digging up the same ground twice. For cable sizes and routes, see [cabling for EV chargers](/services/ev-charger-cabling/).

## Bollards, wheel stops and kerbs

A pedestal or DC charger stands at bumper height in a parking bay, and one reversing car can put it out of service. So we protect it:

- **Steel bollards,** filled with concrete and set in concrete footings, in front of DC cabinets and at the corners of islands.
- **Wheel stops,** placed so the car stops short of the charger while the charging cable still reaches its port.
- **Kerbs or a raised island** around a group of chargers, which also lifts them clear of surface water.

State safety rules ask for this too. Maharashtra's Chief Electrical Inspector, for example, asks for charging points to be protected from damage by vehicles with a raised island, kerbs, bollards or metal barriers, for socket-outlets at least 800 mm above the highest flood level, and for charging bays to be signed and marked on the ground ([safety advisory for EV charging stations](https://www.ceimah.in/uploads/cie/gallery/media/283_CEI%20_%20Safety%20Advisory_%20Standard%20Operating%20Procedure%20for%20EV%20Charging%20Stations%20%28EVCS%29%20across%20Maharashtra_organized.pdf)).

## Canopies

Many chargers are built for outdoor use, so a canopy is not always needed. It earns its place where drivers plug in and wait under sun and rain, where screens are hard to read in the afternoon glare, and where you want the station to be seen from the road.

- **Structure:** steel columns and rafters, galvanised or painted against rust, on concrete foundations.
- **Roof:** profiled metal sheet, polycarbonate, or solar panels.
- **Height:** clear height for the tallest vehicle that will use the bays, including buses and trucks at depots, and room for the charger's cable management.
- **Wind:** a canopy roof catches the wind like a sail, so the structure and its foundations are designed for your area's wind speed, using IS 875 (Part 3), the Indian code for wind loads, including the uplift a storm puts on an open roof.
- **Rainwater:** gutters and downpipes, so water doesn't pour onto the chargers or the drivers.
- **Lighting:** lights fixed under the canopy, for safe use at night.

A **solar canopy** uses the panels themselves as the roof, so the shade over your bays also makes power. See [solar installation](/services/solar-installation/) and [solar EV charging](/solar-ev-charging/).

## Floor marking, signs and lighting

- **Bay marking:** lines and an EV symbol on each bay, in paint that stands up to tyres and weather.
- **EV-only signs,** so petrol and diesel cars don't block the bays.
- **A notice at each charger** showing which vehicles and connectors it serves, such as CCS2 for cars, or the connectors for 2-wheelers and 3-wheelers.
- **Direction signs** from the gate, and height signs on canopies.
- **Lighting** over the bays and the walkway, so drivers can see the connector and feel safe at night.

## Drainage and flood height

In many Indian cities, parking areas flood in the monsoon, and water in a charger or a cable joint means a fault, a tripped supply or a shock risk. So:

- we ask about past waterlogging at the survey and set plinth heights above the highest level the site has seen
- bays slope so water runs away from chargers and DB boxes, with a drain where it collects
- draw pits are drained or sealed, so water doesn't sit around cable joints
- in basements, DB boxes and joints go above the flood line and cable entries stay sealed
