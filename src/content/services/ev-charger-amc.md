## Why EV chargers need maintenance

An EV charger works hard. It carries high current for hours, often outdoors in summer heat, dust and monsoon rain, and its gun is plugged in and out many times a day. Small faults build up quietly until the charger trips, shows an error or stops starting sessions.

- **Uptime is revenue.** A public charger that is down earns nothing, and a driver who finds it broken goes elsewhere. For a fleet, a dead charger means vehicles that aren't ready for the next shift.
- **Safety.** Terminations work loose and heat up, connector pins wear and scorch, earth pits dry out, and an RCD that is never tested may not trip when it should. Regular EV charger maintenance helps find these problems while they are still small.
- **Warranty.** Some charger warranties depend on maintenance done as the maker's manual says, with records to show it. An AMC builds those records, visit by visit.

## What a preventive maintenance visit checks

Each visit follows the charger maker's manual first, then your own checklist if you have one. The tests are the ones we use at [commissioning](/services/ev-charger-commissioning/), so each reading can be compared with the values recorded at handover.

| Check | What we look at |
|---|---|
| **Visual and physical** | The charger body, mounting or pedestal, cable entries and glands, covers, seals and labels, noting any rust, water marks, cracks or impact damage |
| **Cleaning** | Dust off the cabinet, display and holster. On DC chargers, air filters and vents cleaned, or filters changed where the manual asks, so the fans can cool the power modules |
| **Guns, cables and connectors** | Cuts, kinks or crushed sections in the cable, burn marks or discoloured pins, and the latch that holds the gun in the car's inlet |
| **Terminations** | Tightness at the DB box and charger, to the torque the maker gives, with any sign of overheating recorded |
| **DB box and protection** | The MCB and its rating, an RCD trip test with an RCD tester, and the status window on the surge protection device (SPD) |
| **Earth** | The earth pit's resistance, measured with an earth resistance tester, and earth continuity from the charger to the earth bar |
| **Insulation resistance** | The supply cable where needed, such as yearly or after water gets in, with the charger's electronics and the SPD disconnected first |
| **Supply** | Voltage on each phase, and neutral-to-earth voltage, against the maker's limits |
| **Display, RFID and emergency stop** | The screen is readable, the RFID reader accepts a card, and the emergency stop works and resets |
| **Network and OCPP** | The SIM, Wi-Fi or wired link and its signal, and the charger showing online in its CMS or app, with the right status for each gun |
| **Firmware** | The version recorded, and updated as the maker advises, with your go-ahead on a networked charger |
| **Signage and bay** | Signs, floor marking, bollards, lighting, the cable hanger, and any water pooling in the bay |

Earth resistance is highest in the dry season, so test it then at least once a year. See how we test a pit on [earthing for EV chargers](/services/ev-charger-earthing/).

## A report after every visit

After each visit you get a report for every charger:

- **Test values:** each reading against its target and against the last visit, so a slow change, such as an earth that rises every summer, shows up early.
- **Photos:** the charger and its nameplate, the gun pins, the DB box with its cover open, the SPD indicator and the earth pit chamber.
- **Findings:** what we found, what we fixed on the visit, and what needs your decision or the maker's attention.
- **Settings:** the firmware version and the network status.

Operators can have it in their own format, with their own photo list and file names.

## When a charger faults

A charger can still fault between planned visits. When one does, send the error code and photos of the charger's screen and its DB box on WhatsApp. A technician then checks the supply, protection, earth, terminations, network and settings to find the cause.

- **We fix faults on the installation side,** such as a loose or burnt termination, a failed MCB or SPD, a high earth resistance, a damaged supply cable, a lost network connection or a wrong setting.
- **Faults inside the charger,** such as a failed power module or control board, go to its maker under the warranty. We give the maker our photos and test results, so their engineer knows what has already been ruled out.

## Types of contract and what they include

Each contract is agreed with you, and what it includes is written into it:

- **Chargers and sites:** every charger covered, listed by site, make, model and serial number.
- **Visit frequency:** for example quarterly or every six months, based on the maker's manual, how busy the charger is and how dusty the site is.
- **Labour only, or labour plus agreed spares:** visits only, with any parts quoted when they are needed, or visits plus an agreed list of spares, such as DB box parts. Parts inside the charger come from its maker.
- **Fault visits:** whether they are part of the contract or arranged as needed.
- **Checklist and report:** ours, or your own format.

## Who our AMC is for

- **Charge point operators** with many sites across states: one checklist and one report format in every city, so charging station maintenance is done the same way at every site. See [charge point operators](/for/charge-point-operators/).
- **Businesses:** offices, hotels, malls, hospitals and fuel stations with chargers for staff, guests or the public.
- **Housing societies:** shared chargers in common parking, with a report the managing committee can keep on record. See [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/).
- **Fleets:** depot chargers for cabs, delivery vehicles, e-rickshaws and buses.
- **Homes:** a home wall box needs less. A yearly visit to test the earth and the RCD, and check the gun, is usually enough, unless its manual asks for more.

## Chargers of any brand

We maintain AC, DC and light-EV chargers of any brand, whether we installed them or another company did.

Some makers' warranties require their own service team for certain work, such as anything inside the charger, so check your warranty terms before you sign any AMC. We work within them: work the warranty keeps for the maker stays with the maker, and we look after the rest of the installation.

## Monitoring with Proline CMS (optional)

An AMC does not need Proline CMS. We maintain chargers on any CMS or app, and chargers with no network at all.

If your chargers run on [Proline CMS](/cms/), it shows the live status of every gun, raises alerts with deadlines and tracks faults as tickets with step-by-step checklists. That helps spot a fault early, and a ticket records the notes, photos and parts used for each fault.
