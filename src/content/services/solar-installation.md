## What our solar work covers

- **Check the site:** your electricity bills, sanctioned load, roof or parking area, shade, and the chargers you have or plan.
- **Size and design:** the system size, the panel layout, the inverter, cable routes and protection.
- **Supply:** panels, inverters, mounting structures and cable, and for hybrid systems, batteries of different brands.
- **Build:** the structure on your roof or a new carport, then the panels, DC and AC cabling, distribution boxes, earthing and a lightning arrester.
- **Connect and test:** to your main panel or LT panel, with every string tested and the inverter's settings checked before handover.

## Sizing a system for your load and your chargers

We start from your last 12 months of electricity bills, your sanctioned load and the roof or parking you can give to panels.

- **Daytime use.** Panels make power only in daylight, so the best fit is usually what your building uses by day. Units used on site replace units you would buy; units you export are credited under your state's rules.
- **A rule of thumb.** 1 kWp of panels makes about 4 to 5.5 units on a clear sunny day and needs about 10 sq m of shade-free roof ([MSEDCL rooftop solar FAQ](https://www.mahadiscom.in/ismart/media/faq.pdf)). Hazy, cloudy and monsoon days make less.
- **Limits.** Your sanctioned load and your state's rules cap the size you can connect, especially under net metering.
- **Your chargers.** Add the units your chargers use in daylight. A 50 kWp system, on about 500 sq m of shade-free roof, makes roughly 200 to 275 units on a clear day, about what one 60 kW DC charger delivers in four hours at full power. On most sites solar covers part of the charging, not all of it.

## On-grid or hybrid with batteries

| | On-grid | Hybrid, with batteries |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Panels feed your building first; spare power goes to the grid where your DISCOM allows it | The same, plus a battery stores power for later |
| In a power cut | Switches off, so it can't feed lines under repair | The battery keeps chosen loads running |
| Cost and upkeep | Lower: no batteries to buy or replace | Higher: batteries add cost and wear out over the years |
| Suits | Sites with a steady supply and a daytime load | Sites with power cuts, evening load, or solar to use after sunset |

For a business with a daytime load and a steady supply, on-grid is usually the simpler choice. Hybrid makes sense where power cuts hurt, or where solar power needs to be stored for the evening, such as charging after sunset. See [inverter and solar batteries](/batteries/inverter-solar-batteries/) and, for charging stations, [BESS for EV charging](/batteries/bess-for-ev-charging/).

## Roofs, sheds and carports

- **Flat RCC roofs:** a galvanised steel or aluminium structure, held down with ballast blocks or chemical anchors so the roof's waterproofing isn't broken, and raised where you want to use the roof underneath.
- **Metal-sheet sheds:** rails clamped to the sheet's profile, once we have checked that the sheet and the purlins under it can carry the load and have years of life left.
- **Carports and canopies:** steel columns on concrete foundations, with the panels as the roof over your parking. They suit sites with more parking than roof, and they shade the cars and chargers below. See [civil work](/services/ev-charging-civil-work/).

A panel array catches the wind like a sail, so every structure is designed for your area's wind speed, using IS 875 (Part 3), the Indian code for wind loads. The layout leaves walkways for safe cleaning, and older roofs get a check of what they can carry before panels go on.

## Net metering and gross metering

How your solar power is billed depends on your state:

- **Net metering:** a bi-directional meter records what you import and export, and you pay for the net units.
- **Net billing:** exported units are credited at a set rate against your bill, and imported units are charged at your tariff.
- **Gross metering:** all the solar power is exported through its own meter and paid for at a set rate, and you pay for everything you use at your tariff.

Under the Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, as amended in 2021, net metering is allowed for systems up to 500 kW or your sanctioned load, whichever is lower, with net billing or gross metering above that, and each state's electricity regulator sets the details ([Power Line, July 2021](https://powerline.net.in/2021/07/02/mop-notifies-electricity-right-of-consumers-amendment-rules-2021/)). So the schemes on offer, the size limits and the paperwork differ from state to state, and the rate for exported units can be much lower than what you pay for a unit. Check with your DISCOM before the system is sized.

The application goes to your DISCOM in your name. We tell you what to prepare and give you the technical details it asks for, such as the panel and inverter datasheets.

## Solar at EV charging stations

The Ministry of Power's 2024 charging guidelines suggest a single-part tariff for public charging stations, capped at the average cost of supply until 31 March 2028, 30% cheaper in solar hours (0.7 times the tariff) and 30% dearer outside them (1.3 times) ([Lok Sabha answer, Aug 2025](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2994845/1/AU3099_jxi0H3.pdf)). Your state's regulator decides how this applies, in its tariff order. Where it applies, it changes the sums for solar at a public station:

- Grid power in solar hours already costs 0.7 times the tariff, so each unit your panels make by day saves less than it would on a flat tariff.
- Charging after sunset costs 1.3 times the tariff, so solar power stored in a battery by day and used in the evening is worth more. Whether that covers the battery's cost depends on how busy your evenings are and how long the battery lasts; see [BESS for EV charging](/batteries/bess-for-ev-charging/).
- Cheaper grid power in solar hours also helps fleets that can move their charging to the middle of the day, with or without panels.

Solar can still earn its place at a station: it covers the site's own daytime load, a solar canopy shades the bays and makes the station easy to spot, and drivers can see that part of their charge comes from the sun. For the full picture, see [solar EV charging](/solar-ev-charging/).

## Protection, earthing and handover

- **DC side:** a DC distribution box with fuses or breakers, an isolator and surge protection between the panels and the inverter.
- **AC side:** an AC distribution box with a breaker, an isolator and surge protection between the inverter and your panel.
- **Earthing:** separate earth pits for the panel structure, the inverter and the lightning arrester, built and tested the way we do [earthing for EV chargers](/services/ev-charger-earthing/).
- **Tests:** the polarity and open-circuit voltage of each string, insulation resistance, earth resistance, and a check that the inverter shuts down when the grid goes off.
- **Handover:** the test results, photos of the work, and the inverter's monitoring app set up so you can see each day's generation.

Panels need regular cleaning, because dust cuts their output, especially in North Indian summers, and a yearly check of connections, the structure and the earthing. We plan a water point and safe walkways into the design so this is easy to do.
