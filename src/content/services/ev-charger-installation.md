## What our EV charger installation covers

Installing a charger is more than fixing a box to a wall. The charger is only as safe as the supply, cable, protection and earth behind it, so we take care of the whole electrical path, from your meter or panel to the plug.

- **Check the site.** Your sanctioned load, phase, existing wiring and earth, the parking spot and the cable route.
- **Supply the materials.** Copper or aluminium cable sized for the charger's current and the run length, conduit or cable tray, glands and lugs.
- **Fit the DB box.** A dedicated distribution box with the MCB, RCD or RCBO and surge protection the charger maker specifies.
- **Make the earth.** A dedicated earth pit, pipe or chemical, tested for resistance and connected to the charger.
- **Mount the charger.** On a wall bracket or a pedestal, at the right height, with cable management and, outdoors, the right weather protection.
- **Do the civil work.** Pedestal foundations, cable trenches, bollards and canopies where the site needs them.
- **Commission and hand over.** Insulation, earth and protection tests, power-up, app or OCPP setup, a test charge, and a handover pack with results and photos.

## What we follow on every job

Every charger maker publishes an installation manual, and that manual is our first rule: cable size, breaker rating, RCD type and earthing are set to what it asks for. Beyond the manual, our work follows India's electrical safety rules from the Central Electricity Authority and the Indian Standards that apply, such as IS 17017 for EV charging systems, IS 732 for wiring and IS 3043 for earthing.

In practice that means:

- **The right residual-current protection.** Many AC chargers need a Type A RCD with DC fault detection, or a Type B RCD. Fitting the wrong type is a common mistake in EV charger installations.
- **Cable sized for continuous load.** A charger draws its full current for hours, so the cable is chosen for that, with the voltage drop over the run kept in check.
- **A tested earth.** We measure earth resistance and record it in the handover pack, not just assume it.
- **Neat, protected cable routes.** Conduit, tray or armoured cable where the cable can be hit, crushed or rained on.
