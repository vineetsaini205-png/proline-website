## What a site survey checks

Most of the problems that hold up a charger installation can be seen on site before anything is bought: a sanctioned load with no room left, a long cable route, a weak earth, no mobile signal in the basement. A survey finds them while they are still easy to plan around. Our engineer checks:

| What we check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Supply type** | Single or three phase, LT or HT. 11 and 22 kW AC chargers and DC chargers need three phase, and large DC sites may need HT. |
| **Sanctioned load** | Read from your electricity bill and compared with what already runs, to see whether the charger fits or a load increase or separate EV connection is needed. |
| **Phases and voltage** | The voltage and load on each phase, and which phase can take a single-phase charger. |
| **Meter and main DB** | Where the charger's supply can be taken from, whether there is room for a new breaker, and the state of the existing wiring. |
| **Existing earth** | Tested with an earth resistance tester, to see whether it can be used or a new pit is needed. On larger sites, a soil resistivity test shows how deep a new earth must go. |
| **Cable route and distance** | Measured from the supply to the charger spot, with how the cable can run: on a wall, in conduit, on tray, along a basement ceiling or in a trench. The length sets the cable size. |
| **Parking and charger spot** | The bays, a wall or pedestal position where the charging cable reaches the car's port, and exposure to rain, sun and flooding. |
| **Civil work** | Foundations, trenches, road crossings, bollards or a canopy, and the surface to cut and make good. |
| **Mobile signal** | Networked chargers reach their CMS over OCPP, through a 4G SIM, Wi-Fi or a wired link, so we check the signal where the charger will stand. |
| **Permissions** | Whether the job needs a society or landlord NOC, a load increase or new connection, or permission to cross a road. |

For a DC charger or a charging station, the survey also looks at the space for an LT panel or a transformer, the bay layout, and the room each charger needs around it for airflow and service.

## What the survey report contains

After the visit, you get a survey report that sets out what we found and what the job needs:

- the site's supply, sanctioned load, meter and DB, with photos
- the measurements: voltage, existing earth resistance, cable route length and the mobile signal at the charger spot
- the recommended charger position and, for larger sites, a layout of bays, chargers and panels
- the cable size and route, the DB box or panel, and the earthing the job needs
- the civil work: foundation, trench, bollards or canopy
- the permissions and documents to prepare, such as a society NOC or a load increase
- anything that needs your decision, such as a longer route or a separate EV connection

With the report comes one clear quote for the whole job, so you know what you are agreeing to before any work starts.

## When photos on WhatsApp are enough

Not every job needs a visit. For most homes, and for a single AC charger close to the main DB, photos are enough to plan the work and quote for it. Send these on WhatsApp to +91 73038 87364:

1. The electricity meter and main DB, with its door open so the breakers show (don't remove any cover)
2. The latest electricity bill, showing the sanctioned load and connection type
3. The parking spot and the wall or place where the charger will go
4. The route from the meter or DB to the charger, or a rough distance
5. The charger's label or datasheet, if you already have one

Book a survey instead when:

- you plan a DC fast charger, several chargers or a [charging station](/services/ev-charging-station-setup/)
- the supply may need a load increase, a new connection or HT
- the cable route is long, runs through a basement, or crosses a driveway or road
- the charger needs a foundation, a trench or a canopy
- the charger will stand where the mobile signal is in doubt, such as a basement
- the photos leave questions we can't answer from a distance

## How to prepare for the visit

- Someone on site who can open the meter room, the main DB and the parking area
- The latest electricity bill
- Site or parking drawings, if you have them
- The charger's model or datasheet, if you have chosen one
- In a housing society, the contact who handles the NOC, and the cable route the society allows
