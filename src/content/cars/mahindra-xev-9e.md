## Charging times by battery and charger

Mahindra's XEV 9e leaflet lists the two wall chargers but not their AC charging times. The XEV 9e uses the same 59 kWh and 79 kWh batteries as the BE 6, for which Mahindra publishes these 0–100% times:

| Battery | 7.2 kW charger | 11.2 kW charger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 59 kWh | 8.7 hours | 6 hours |
| 79 kWh | 11.7 hours | 8 hours |

On public DC fast chargers, Mahindra quotes 20–80% in 20 minutes for the XEV 9e: on a 140 kW charger for the 59 kWh battery and a 175 kW charger for the 79 kWh battery.

## 7.2 kW or 11.2 kW: how to choose

The XEV 9e charges faster on the 11.2 kW charger, so the choice is a real one:

- **7.2 kW charger.** Runs on a normal single-phase supply and draws about 32 A. Even the 79 kWh battery charges fully overnight. This suits most homes.
- **11.2 kW charger.** Needs a three-phase supply and saves a few hours on a full charge. It suits homes and offices that already have three-phase power, or owners who drive long distances most days.

## What Mahindra supplies

Mahindra's leaflet says the XEV 9e is billed with either a 7.2 kW or an 11.2 kW charger at extra cost, and its FAQ says the starting price does not include the charger or installation costs. The Cineluxe Edition includes a 3.3 kW portable charger as standard. If the dealer arranges installation, ask what it includes; we can do the part it leaves out.

## Single-phase or three-phase?

An 11 kW-class charger takes its power across three phases, so it needs a three-phase connection. On a normal single-phase supply, a 7.2–7.4 kW wall box is the one that fits: it draws about 32 A while it charges. If you want the faster charger and have single-phase power, a three-phase connection means an application to your DISCOM and often a load increase. We check your meter and bill before you decide, and for most owners the slower charger is enough, because the car is parked at home for longer than its charging time.

## Before you buy a wall box

- **Match the car.** A charger only charges as fast as the car accepts on AC. The times above are the ones Mahindra quotes, so a bigger charger than those figures assume costs more without charging faster.
- **Check the supply.** Single-phase homes suit a 7.2–7.4 kW wall box; 11 kW-class chargers need three-phase.
- **Plan the cable.** Measure from your meter or DB to where the car's charging port sits when it is parked. The cable size grows with the distance.
- **Keep the records.** We hand over the test results and photos of the installation, which help with any warranty claim later.

## How we install it

Every wall box goes on its own circuit from your meter or DB, with an MCB and the right type of RCD in a [DB box](/services/ev-charger-db-box/), and a [tested earth](/services/ev-charger-earthing/). If the car's portable charger is your backup, use it only from a dedicated 15/16 A socket with its own breaker, RCD and earth, not a shared plug point. The steps are below.

## What to send us

To tell you which charger fits and give you one clear quote, we need:

1. A photo of your electricity meter, which shows whether your supply is single-phase or three-phase.
2. A recent electricity bill, which shows your sanctioned load.
3. Photos of the main DB and the parking spot, and the path a cable would take between them.
4. Which Mahindra XEV 9e battery you have, and the charger Mahindra supplied, if any.

## In a housing society

Most XEV 9e owners in apartments charge in the basement. The cable usually runs from your own meter in the meter room to your parking slot, and the society's permission comes first. Our page for [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/) explains the usual approvals and how shared chargers can work.
