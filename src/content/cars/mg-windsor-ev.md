## Which charger the Windsor needs

MG publishes two charging figures for the Windsor Pro with its 52.9 kWh battery:

| Way to charge | What you need | MG's figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7.4 kW AC charger | The 7.4 kW wall box MG includes with the Essence Pro and Exclusive Pro, on its own circuit | 10–100% in about 9.5 hours |
| DC fast charger | A public 60 kW DC fast charger | 20–80% in about 50 minutes |

So the answer for most owners is simple: use the **7.4 kW AC charger** at home overnight, and DC fast chargers on long trips. A 7.4 kW wall box runs on a normal single-phase supply. A bigger AC charger costs more and does not help, because an AC charger can only charge as fast as the car accepts, and MG's home figure is for 7.4 kW.

## What a good installation needs

The charger is only as safe as the circuit behind it. A 7.4 kW wall box draws about 32 A for hours at a time, so it needs:

- **Its own circuit.** A cable sized for the current and the distance, straight from your meter or main DB, never shared with sockets or lights.
- **The right protection.** An MCB and an RCD of the type the charger maker asks for, in a [DB box](/services/ev-charger-db-box/) near the charger.
- **A good earth.** Most makers ask for a dedicated earth with a low, tested resistance. We test the earth you have and add a [new earth pit](/services/ev-charger-earthing/) if it falls short.
- **A safe route.** Cable in conduit or a tray, clear of where cars drive, and a charger placed where its cable reaches the Windsor's charging port without stretching.

## Will your electricity connection take it?

Whether your connection can carry another 32 A depends on your sanctioned load and what else runs while you charge, such as air conditioners or a geyser. We check this from your electricity bill and a photo of the meter. If the load is tight, the options are a load increase from your DISCOM, a lower current setting on the charger, or a charger with load management.

## What to send us

For most homes, photos are enough to plan the job and give you one clear quote:

1. Your electricity meter and the main DB, with the covers on.
2. A recent electricity bill, which shows your sanctioned load.
3. The parking spot, and the path a cable would take from the meter.
4. The charger's box or label, if MG has already given it to you.

If the photos leave questions, such as a long basement route, we suggest a paid site survey before we quote.

## In a housing society

In an apartment, the cable usually runs from your own meter in the meter room to your parking slot, and the society's permission comes first. Societies usually want the charger model, the cable route and how the power is metered. We give you these details for the application. See our page for [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/).
