## Which charger the Nexon.ev needs

The Nexon.ev charges at home in three ways. Tata publishes the time for each, so you can pick by how far you drive.

| Way to charge | What you need | Tata's figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 A socket | A dedicated 15/16 A socket with its own breaker and earthing, used with the 3.3 kW charger box | About 17 hours 36 minutes for a full charge |
| AC wall box | A 7.2–7.4 kW wall box on its own circuit | 10–100% in 6 hours 36 minutes |
| DC fast charger | A public 60 kW DC charger (CCS2) | 10–80% in about 40 minutes |

For most owners the answer is a **7.2–7.4 kW AC wall box** at home, with DC fast charging on trips. A 7.4 kW single-phase charger is the right size: Tata's fastest home option for the Nexon.ev is 7.2 kW AC, so an 11 kW or 22 kW charger costs more and charges the car no faster.

If you drive short distances most days, the 3.3 kW charger box on a proper socket can be enough, because it has the whole night to top up. The socket must be a dedicated 15/16 A circuit, not a shared plug point.

## What Tata's complimentary installation covers, and what it leaves out

Tata offers a complimentary home-charger installation with eligible cars. Its charging support page says that installation does **not** include earthing or civil work. Those are the two things that most often hold up a safe installation:

- **Earthing.** Many homes and older buildings don't have an earth good enough for an EV charger. We dig and test a new earth pit, or test and connect to a good existing one.
- **Civil work.** A trench across a driveway, a core cut through a wall, or a cable tray along a basement ceiling.
- **Long cable runs.** When the parking spot is far from the meter, the cable size has to grow with the distance.

## Will your electricity connection take it?

A 7.2–7.4 kW wall box runs on a normal single-phase supply and draws about 32 A while it charges. Whether your connection can carry that depends on your sanctioned load and what else runs at the same time, such as air conditioners or a geyser. We check this from your electricity bill and photos of the meter. If the load is tight, the options are a load increase from your DISCOM, a lower current setting on the charger, or a charger with load management.

## In a housing society

Most Nexon.ev owners in apartments charge in the basement. The cable usually runs from your own meter in the meter room to your parking slot, and the society's permission comes first. Our page for [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/) explains the usual approvals and how shared chargers can work.
