## Charging times by battery and charger

Hyundai publishes 10–100% charging times for both Creta Electric batteries:

| Battery | 7.4 kW wall box | 11 kW wall box |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 42 kWh | About 6 hours | About 4 hours |
| 51.4 kWh | About 7 hours 15 minutes | About 4 hours 50 minutes |

On a DC fast charger, Hyundai's specification page gives 10–80% in about 39 minutes. The car uses the CCS2 connector, the usual one at public car fast chargers in India.

## 7.4 kW or 11 kW: how to choose

The Creta Electric charges faster on an 11 kW wall box than on a 7.4 kW one, so the choice is a real one. It comes down to your supply and how you use the car:

- **7.4 kW wall box.** Runs on a normal single-phase supply and draws about 32 A. Even the bigger battery charges fully overnight. This suits most homes.
- **11 kW wall box.** Needs a three-phase supply. It saves about two and a half hours on a full charge of the 51.4 kWh battery. It suits homes and offices that already have three-phase power, owners who drive long distances most days, or a car that is shared.

If you have a single-phase connection, three-phase means an application to your DISCOM and often a load increase. For many owners the 7.4 kW wall box is enough, because the car sits at home longer than it takes to charge.

## What the installation involves

Either wall box needs its own circuit from your meter or main DB, a cable sized for the current and the distance, an MCB and RCD in a [DB box](/services/ev-charger-db-box/), and a [tested earth](/services/ev-charger-earthing/). For an 11 kW wall box, the cable and protection are four-pole, and the load is shared across the three phases. We check the sanctioned load on your bill before we quote, and mount the wall box where its cable reaches the car's charging port without stretching.

## What to send us

To tell you which wall box fits and give you one clear quote, send:

1. A photo of your electricity meter, which shows whether your supply is single-phase or three-phase.
2. A recent electricity bill, which shows your sanctioned load.
3. Photos of the main DB and the parking spot, and the path a cable would take between them.
4. Which Creta Electric battery you have, and the wall box, if you have bought one.

## In a housing society

In an apartment, the cable usually runs from your own meter in the meter room to your parking slot, after the society agrees. If your meter is single-phase, the 7.4 kW wall box is the one that fits. Our page for [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/) explains the usual approvals.
