## Charging times by battery and charger

Mahindra publishes 0–100% charging times for each BE 6 battery on its two wall chargers:

| Battery | 7.2 kW charger | 11.2 kW charger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 59 kWh | 8.7 hours | 6 hours |
| 70 kWh | 10.2 hours | 7 hours |
| 79 kWh | 11.7 hours | 8 hours |

On public DC fast chargers, Mahindra says every BE 6 battery goes from 20 to 80% in about 20 minutes, with DC charging of up to 180 kW. The car uses the CCS2 connector, the usual connector at public car fast chargers in India.

## 7.2 kW or 11.2 kW: how to choose

Unlike many cars, the BE 6 charges faster on an 11.2 kW charger than on a 7.2 kW one, so the choice is a real one. It comes down to your supply and how you drive:

- **7.2 kW charger.** Runs on a normal single-phase supply and draws about 32 A. Even the 79 kWh battery charges fully overnight. This suits most homes.
- **11.2 kW charger.** Needs a three-phase supply. It saves about three hours on a full charge of the bigger batteries. It suits homes and offices that already have three-phase power, owners who drive long distances most days, or a car that is shared.

If you have a single-phase connection, a three-phase one means an application to your DISCOM and often a load increase. For many owners the 7.2 kW charger is enough, because the car sits at home for longer than its charging time.

The 3.3 kW portable charger that comes with the car is a backup. Use it only from a dedicated 15/16 A socket with its own breaker, RCD and earth.

## What the installation involves

Both wall chargers need their own circuit from your meter or main DB, a cable sized for the current and the distance, an MCB and RCD in a [DB box](/services/ev-charger-db-box/), and a [tested earth](/services/ev-charger-earthing/). For the 11.2 kW charger, the cable and protection are four-pole, and the load is shared across the three phases. We check the sanctioned load on your bill before we quote.

## What to send us

To tell you which charger fits and give you one clear quote, we need:

1. A photo of your electricity meter, which shows whether your supply is single-phase or three-phase.
2. A recent electricity bill, which shows your sanctioned load.
3. Photos of the main DB and the parking spot, and the path a cable would take between them.
4. Which BE 6 battery you have, and the charger Mahindra supplied, if any.

## In a housing society

In an apartment, the cable usually runs from your own meter in the meter room to your parking slot, after the society agrees. If your meter is single-phase, the 7.2 kW charger is the one that fits. Our page for [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/) explains the usual approvals.
