## Which charger the Punch.ev needs

Tata gives the new Punch.ev two home charging options, and it fast-charges on public DC chargers.

| Way to charge | What you need | What Tata says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.3 kW AC charger | A dedicated 15/16 A socket with its own breaker, RCD and earth | The standard home charging option |
| 7.2 kW AC unit | A 7.2–7.4 kW wall box on its own circuit | An optional home charging unit |
| DC fast charger | A public DC fast charger (the car supports up to 65 kW) | 20–80% in 26 minutes |

The new Punch.ev comes with a 30 kWh or a 40 kWh battery. The 7.2 kW unit gives a little over twice the power of the 3.3 kW charger, so it fills the same battery in a little under half the time. Tata's home options stop at 7.2 kW, so a 7.4 kW single-phase wall box is the right size if you want something faster than the standard charger. Ask us before you buy an 11 kW or 22 kW charger: an AC charger can only charge as fast as the car accepts.

## 3.3 kW or 7.2 kW: how to choose

- **The 3.3 kW charger** suits you if you park at home every night and drive short distances most days. It plugs into a socket, so the main job is a safe, dedicated 15/16 A point near your parking spot.
- **A 7.2–7.4 kW wall box** suits you if you drive long distances, share the car, or come home with little time before the next trip. It is fixed to the wall on its own circuit and has its own app on many models.

Both need a good earth and the right protection. The difference is mostly the size of the cable and breaker, and whether your sanctioned load has room for the extra power.

## Will your electricity connection take it?

The 3.3 kW charger draws about 15 A and a 7.2–7.4 kW wall box about 32 A, both on a normal single-phase supply. Whether your connection can carry that depends on your sanctioned load and what else runs while you charge. We check this from your electricity bill and a photo of the meter. If the load is tight, the options are a load increase, a lower current setting on the charger, or a charger with load management.

## What Tata's complimentary installation leaves out

Tata's charging support page says its complimentary home-charger installation does **not** include earthing or civil work. Those are often the hardest parts of the job:

- **Earthing.** We test the earth you have and add a [new earth pit](/services/ev-charger-earthing/) if it falls short.
- **Civil work.** A trench, a core cut through a wall or a cable tray in a basement.
- **Long cable runs.** The cable size grows with the distance from your meter to the car.

## In a housing society

In an apartment, the cable usually runs from your own meter in the meter room to your parking slot, and the society's permission comes first. Our page for [housing societies](/for/housing-societies/) explains the usual approvals. If the society plans shared chargers instead, we install those too.
